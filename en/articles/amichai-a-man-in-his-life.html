A Man in His Life (1982): Yehuda Amichai’s anti-Kohelet

The third chapter of Ecclesiastes (or, in Hebrew, Kohelet) is one of the most hauntingly beautiful pieces of world literature. You can hear some of the words of Ecclesiastes while watching Forrest Gump, at the end of the March on the Pentagon rally scene, in Peter Seeger’s folk song Turn! Turn! Turn! performed by The Byrds.

To everything, turn, turn, turn
There is a season, turn, turn, turn
And a time to every purpose under heaven
A time to be born, a time to die
A time to plant, a time to reap
A time to kill, a time to heal
A time to laugh, a time to weep
To everything, turn, turn, turn
There is a season, turn, turn, turn
And a time to every purpose, under heaven
A time to build up, a time to break down
A time to dance, a time to mourn
A time to cast away stones
A time to gather stones together
To everything, turn, turn, turn
There is a season, turn, turn, turn
And a time to every purpose under heaven
A time of love, a time of hate
A time of war, a time of peace
A time you may embrace
A time to refrain from embracing
To everything, turn, turn, turn
There is a season, turn, turn, turn
And a time to every purpose under heaven
A time to gain, a time to lose
A time to rend, a time to sew
A time for love, a time for hate
A time for peace, I swear it’s not too late

Seeger’s lyrics are almost entirely the same as the verses from Ecclesiastes (“paraphrased enough to make them his own”), but the pacifist last line

A time for peace, I swear it’s not too late
is his own personal interpolation. He wrote the song in 1959, and its most famous renditions (my personal favourite is Judy Collins’)1 are from the immediately following years, so it makes sense that Ecclesiastes’ words are in dialogue with the Vietnam war. One of the themes of Ecclesiastes is also Man’s relationship with History.

Israeli poet and writer Yehuda Amichai wrote his own reply to Kohelet in his masterpiece poetry collection The Hour of Grace, published in 1982, in the poem A Man in His Life.

A man in his life has no time
to have time for everything.
And he doesn’t have a moment to have a moment
for every matter. Kohelet was wrong when saying so.

A man should hate and love at the same time,
with the same eyes cry and with the same eyes laugh
with the same hands cast stones
and with the same hands gather them,
make love in war and war in love.

And hate and forgive and forget and remember
and order and confuse and eat and digest
what long History
does during many years.

A man in his life has no time.
When he loses he finds,
when he finds he forgets,
when he forgets he loves
and when he loves he starts to forget.

And his soul is experienced
and his soul is very professional
only his body is an amateur
always. Trying and making mistakes
not learning and getting confused
drunk and blind in his pleasures and sorrows.

Like figs he will die in autumn
withered, full of himself and sweet,
the leaves covering the earth
and the naked branches already pointing
to the place where there’s time for all.
